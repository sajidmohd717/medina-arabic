Lesson

Read through the grammar explanation, then study the examples.

What is هَذَا?

هَذَا (hādhā) means "this" in English. It is called a demonstrative pronoun — a word you use to point at something near you.

In Arabic, هَذَا is used exclusively with masculine nouns. You will learn the feminine version (هَذِهِ) in Lesson 6.

هَذَا is always used for masculine nouns. It never changes its form.

Arabic has no word for "is"

In English we say: "This is a house."

In Arabic, there is no word for "is". You simply place هَذَا next to the noun:

هَذَا + noun = "This is a [noun]"

So هَذَا بَيْتٌ literally reads "This a house" — but means "This is a house." The "is" is implied.

The -un sound (Tanwīn)

You will notice all the nouns end in the sound -un (written as ٌ — two small damma marks). This is called tanwīn and it means the noun is indefinite — like "a house" rather than "the house". You will learn about definite nouns (the house) in Lesson 3.

Noun + ٌ (tanwīn) = indefinite noun → "a [noun]"

Example sentences

Arabic Transliteration Meaning
هَذَا بَيْتٌ hādhā baytun This is a house.
هَذَا كِتَابٌ hādhā kitābun This is a book.
هَذَا قَلَمٌ hādhā qalamun This is a pen.
هَذَا مَسْجِدٌ hādhā masjidun This is a mosque.
هَذَا بَابٌ hādhā bābun This is a door.
هَذَا طَالِبٌ hādhā ṭālibun This is a student.
